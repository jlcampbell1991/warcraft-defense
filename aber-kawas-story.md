# Aber Kawas: First Palestinian in New York State Senate History

In a night that will be remembered for generations, **Aber Kawas** shattered a historic ceiling, becoming the first Palestinian elected to the New York State Senate. Running on a bold democratic‑socialist platform, Kawas captured **74.5 % of the vote**, a landslide that reflected both his personal charisma and the growing appetite for progressive change in the Empire State.

Born to a Palestinian‑American family in Brooklyn, Kawas grew up hearing stories of displacement, resilience, and the quest for justice. Those narratives shaped his worldview: a belief that public policy should lift the most vulnerable and that representation matters. After earning a degree in political science from SUNY Albany, he spent a decade organizing community housing initiatives, fighting for affordable rent, and championing universal healthcare.

When the incumbent announced retirement, Kawas seized the moment. His campaign slogan—*“Justice for All, Power to the People”*—became a rallying cry across the city’s diverse neighborhoods. He toured the Bronx, Queens, and upstate towns, holding town‑halls in community centers, mosques, and union halls. His policy proposals were unapologetically progressive: a $15 minimum wage, tuition‑free public colleges, a Green New Deal for New York, and a comprehensive criminal‑justice reform package.

The primary was a test of his message. Against a well‑funded moderate, Kawas won the Democratic nomination with 62 % of the vote. In the general election, his coalition—immigrants, students, labor unions, and young progressives—turned out in record numbers. On election night, the precincts reported an overwhelming 74.5 % in his favor, a mandate that echoed far beyond the state legislature.

Kawas’s victory is more than a personal achievement; it signals a shift in New York politics. For the Palestinian diaspora, it is a symbol of inclusion and the power of civic participation. For progressives nationwide, it demonstrates that bold platforms can win big majorities when grounded in community organizing.

As he steps onto the Senate floor, Kawas pledges to “bring the voices of the unheard into every bill, every debate, and every vote.” His historic win reminds us that representation matters, and that the future of New York politics will be written by those who dare to imagine a more just, equitable society.
