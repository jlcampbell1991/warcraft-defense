# Canada recommits to federal funding for news

The federal government of Canada announced today that it will **restore and increase its annual funding for Canadian news media**, reversing a series of cuts made over the past few years. The new package, worth **$250 million over three years**, aims to support local journalism, digital innovation, and investigative reporting across the country.

The decision follows a backlash from media organisations and press‑freedom groups who warned that previous funding reductions were contributing to newsroom closures and a decline in original reporting. Minister of Canadian Heritage **Steven Guilbeault** emphasized that a vibrant press is essential to a healthy democracy, stating, “Canadian stories matter, and we must ensure they are told by strong, independent journalists.”

Under the renewed program, eligible outlets will receive grants based on circulation, digital reach, and commitment to public‑interest reporting. Additional funds are earmarked for training journalists in emerging technologies such as data‑driven storytelling and multimedia production.

Analysts predict the infusion will help stabilise struggling community newspapers and enable larger broadcasters to invest in investigative units. However, some critics argue the funding model still favours established players and call for stricter accountability measures to ensure the money reaches diverse voices.

The move marks a significant policy shift, signalling the government’s recognition of the crucial role news media plays in informing citizens and holding power to account. As Canada navigates a rapidly changing media landscape, the renewed funding could be a turning point for the nation’s press freedom and democratic resilience.
